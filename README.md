# memory-artifact

A Lean 4 model of the Memory Artifact, with machine-checked proofs, built to be cited by the paper. This is the second version. It is made from two records of the desk, written on 2026-09-24:

- `MEMORY_ARTIFACT_definitions_2026-09-24.md`, the 69 terms, each defined only from the ones above it. Every Lean name below maps to a number in that file.
- `MEMORY_ARTIFACT_design_2026-09-24.md`, the design record: sections 2 to 5 (the model), 9 (the theorems), 12 to 13 (the addendum, the append catalogue, the invariants), 14 (the toolkit as a closed list, work and recreation), 15 (the desk's rulings on the first version's findings), 16 (lookup by words), 17 (the theorems of this version), 18 to 18g (geometry, the index and the policy's anchors, what bounds a trace, index epochs as a chain of policy infos).

Where the two disagree the definitions file wins (a desk ruling), and this README lists every place where they did.

The first version (commit `3d0e9ce`) proved the five theorems of section 9 over 55 terms. This version adds terms 56 to 69, the ten tools and their appends, four invariants, lookup by words with a named coverage hypothesis, and theorems T6 to T14. It re-proves the first version's five theorems of section 9 over the changed structures; the first version's `entries_in_closure_of_own_retire` (a stronger reading of Theorem 2) is dropped, because it fails once edges are not entries (ruling 1).

## Build

```text
lake build
```

- Toolchain: `leanprover/lean4:v4.31.0`, pinned in `lean-toolchain`.
- Core library only. No Mathlib, no other package, no network.
- No `sorry`, no `native_decide`, no `axiom`. Every theorem depends on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` at most, which are Lean's own. Check any theorem with `#print axioms MemoryArtifact.total_reachability`; a scan over every constant of the library finds none that depends on `sorryAx` or on any axiom other than those three (reproduce with `lake env lean Scan.lean`; `Scan.lean` is a script at the root, not part of the library; it fails when it finds one, and CI runs it after the build). At the commit of 2026-10-01 the scan covers 3955 constants, of which 2531 are theorem constants of the kernel: 810 are `theorem` declarations of the source, 50 are proposition-valued structure fields, and the rest are generated by the compiler (equation lemmas and the like).
- The concrete witness (`Nonvacuous.lean`) is checked by the kernel with `decide +kernel`, which is Lean's own checker, not a native one.

## The model in one page

A `Memory` is four logs: hippocampus, private store, shared store, toolkit. An `Info` is a `Body` (data, envelope, derivation pointers, a pointer to the info before it in its log, an arrival number) and a hash. A `Hasher` is an injective function from `Content` to hashes: "the same data always has the same hash and different data never share one" (3). The hash covers a `Content`: the data, the envelope and the derivation pointers. The history pointer and the arrival number are the log's structure and are outside the hash (ruling 10). Everything below takes a context `Γ : Ctx` that fixes the hasher, the model's name, the harness's name, the ten tools' names, the knobs (fan-out `k`, keep cap `c`, return cap `cap`, title size), how long a recipe really takes (`recipeTime`), and the ranker and the embedder that lookup by words uses. The policy the ranker runs under is not a parameter: it is an info of the shared store (`Kind.policy`), and the policy in force is the latest one of the log.

`WellFormed Γ m` is twelve invariants, one named conjunct each (1 to 11 and 13; invariant 12, the index, lives outside it, see below). Two of the twelve follow from others and add nothing: invariant 2 from 11, and invariant 8 from 1. `append Γ m l i` checks the local counterparts of the invariants on the one info against the memory it would join, and either returns the memory with the info added to the end of log `l`, or a `Refusal` that names the first invariant it would break. `wellFormed_push_iff` says that, for a well-formed memory, the local checks are exactly the invariants of the memory after the push, so an append is refused exactly when an invariant would break.

A refusal is itself an append: the harness operation `step` leaves a return of kind refusal in the private store (`step_refused`; invariant 8 asks only that such a return has data). `append` returns a value and leaves the memory it was offered untouched; `step` changes the memory by exactly the refusal record (`step_refused`).

A tool call is the second kind of operation. `toolStep Γ m c` appends the experience of the call (kind `call`, first pointer the tool's declaration in the toolkit, on a day of work the task too), then the tool's own appends, then a return (written by the tool, first pointer the call), then a cursor for the page served. The call's own appends go through `append`. The refusal records are placed without that check; they pass it on every well-formed memory (`ok_refusalDraft`), so a well-formed memory stays well-formed whatever the call (`toolStep_wellFormed`). What the model writes is an input of the call: the writing of each link of a `consider` chain is given, not computed.

`startDay Γ m` is what the harness appends at the start of a day: group nodes over the heads while there are more than `k` of them, then the root. `view Γ m` is knowledge (31), computed on the memory `startDay` produces. `root`, `startDay` and `view` are functions of the memory, and so of its four logs.

The operations are `Op.offer l i` (an offer, accepted or refused), `Op.tool c` (a call) and `Op.newDay` (`startDay`). `Derivable Γ m` is the class of memories reached from the empty memory by operations, where an offer is only of what a caller may offer (`Kind.offerableIn`): never a root, a group, or anything only a tool builds (the stop is built by the tool `stop` and, under amendment B, also offered as the individual's stop line). Hydrating, opening a frame, computing the view are reads, not operations.

Invariant 12 is that the vector index is derived from the log and the policy, rebuildable, never authoritative, with no pointer that is not in the log and no reachability through it. In this model the index is not an object: `Memory.derivedIndex` is a function of the log by definition, `Memory.closure`, `thread` and `heads` are typed on the memory alone, and `structural_independent_of_ranker` shows that, for a given memory, the root, the start of a day, the view and the depth are the same under another ranker, embedder or set of tool names (a policy is an info of the log, not a parameter that varies). That is the honest content; see "What is not proved".

## The ten tools

A tool is a name in the toolkit and a function outside the model; its effect on the memory is what `toolStep` appends. Every call records its experience first (kind `call`, first pointer the tool's declaration, then the pointers the call names, then, on a day of work, the task), and ends with a return and a cursor.

| tool       | Lean                  | appends after the call                                                                                                              | return kind                  |
| ---------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| `recall`   | `ToolCall.recall q`   | nothing                                                                                                                             | infos, span, nothing         |
| `reach`    | `ToolCall.reach q`    | nothing                                                                                                                             | infos, span, nothing         |
| `consider` | `ToolCall.consider …` | for each trace, a chain of asides, each opened by the call or by the link before it, with a continues edge to the info it continues | digest                       |
| `keeping`  | `ToolCall.keeping …`  | a keep                                                                                                                              | acknowledgement              |
| `relate`   | `ToolCall.relate …`   | an edge                                                                                                                             | acknowledgement              |
| `file`     | `ToolCall.file …`     | an info of kind filed in the shared store                                                                                           | acknowledgement              |
| `act`      | `ToolCall.act …`      | the recipe named, the data given, and an outcome                                                                                    | acknowledgement (or refusal) |
| `ask`      | `ToolCall.ask …`      | a question                                                                                                                          | acknowledgement              |
| `hand`     | `ToolCall.hand …`     | a hand-over, which ends the day; after it only the night or a stop                                                                  | acknowledgement              |
| `stop`     | `ToolCall.stop`       | a stop, which ends the day; after it only the night or a stop                                                                       | acknowledgement              |

A lookup takes a `Query`: a pointer (`ptr`), a span (`span`) or words (`words`). By a pointer it returns the info the pointer names, in the scope of the tool (the hippocampus for recall, the store for reach), whatever the ranker; by a span, the part of that info's canonical form the span names, one page, with a cursor from which the writer continues; by words, the fusion of a lexical side (the infos whose data holds every word) and the ranker's answer. The call records the query: for words, the words, and its derivation points to the policy the lookup ran under, the latest policy info of the shared store (with no policy in the log, the ranker is not consulted and only the lexical side answers), so the lookup is replayable from the log under its own epoch (`lookup_replayable`).

### Theorem 1: bounded root

```lean
theorem bounded_root_theorem (Γ : Ctx) :
    (∀ m : Memory, (root Γ m).size ≤ Γ.p.rootBound) ∧
    (∀ m : Memory, WellFormed Γ m → WellFormed Γ (startDay Γ m))
```

The root's size is at most `2 + k * (2 + titleCap) + 2 * c`, for every memory, well-formed or not. `Params.hk : 2 ≤ k` (a fan-out below two would not shrink a level) and `titleCap` (the number of tokens of a head's title the root shows) are premises the record does not list.

### Theorem 2: total reachability

The record's sentence, "every entry is reachable from the root in at most depth plus one hops, depth logarithmic in the number of derived entries", is read in four parts, over entries (ruling 1), as the desk accepted for the first version:

1. knowledge is complete: every entry no supersedes edge has retired is in the view (the closure of the root over derivations and relation edges);
2. every head is reached from the root in at most `depth + 1` hops of derivation, through the group tree;
3. the depth is logarithmic: `|heads| ≤ k ^ (depth + 1)`, and unless the depth is zero, `k ^ depth < |heads| ≤ |entries|`;
4. every info of the memory, retired or not, is reached by its name.

```lean
theorem total_reachability (Γ : Ctx) (m : Memory) (hwf : WellFormed Γ m) :
    (∀ x ∈ m.entries, ¬m.retired x → x ∈ view Γ m) ∧
    (∀ x ∈ m.heads, ∃ n ≤ depth Γ m + 1, PtrPath (startDay Γ m) n (root Γ m).hash x.hash) ∧
    (m.heads.length ≤ Γ.p.k ^ (depth Γ m + 1) ∧ (depth Γ m = 0 ∨ Γ.p.k ^ depth Γ m < m.heads.length) ∧
      m.heads.length ≤ m.entries.length) ∧
    (∀ x ∈ m.all, m.resolve x.hash = some x)
```

The literal reading, "every info of the memory is reachable from the root within depth + 1 hops", is false for the catalogue as written, and this is proved, not argued (`literal_reachability_false`, `Witnesses.lean`): in a memory whose only info is a tool declaration the root points to nothing. (In a memory with calls a call points to its tool's declaration, so a declaration can lie in a root's closure; the literal reading fails for every info that nothing the root reaches points to.) The stronger reading "every entry, retired or not" is also not claimed: a retired entry can lie out of reach of the root (an entry retired by a supersedes edge from an info of the toolkit, for instance, whose edge joins nothing the root reaches; `retired_entry_unreachable` in `Nonvacuous.lean`). The extent is larger than these examples suggest: in the witness of `Nonvacuous.lean`, 20 of the 30 infos of the hippocampus, none of them retired, lie outside the closure of the next root (calls that no aside or keep points to, the bookkeeping of an act, relation edges, a stop that points to nothing; measured by the audit of 2026-10-01). The resource premise of section 12 ("the machine holds the record on disk and a closure in memory") and "only time grows" are not modelled: names are distinct because the hash function is injective, and a total model has no clock.

### Theorem 3: determinism

Two harnesses over the same hash function, ranker and knobs that run the same operations reach the same memory, and so compute the same root, the same view and the same lookups (`determinism` states the memory, the root and the view; lookups are functions of the memory). This is by construction: the operations are functions, and a `Harness` is anything that runs the empty list to the empty memory and one more operation as `Op.run`. The content is that the memory itself is fixed by the operations. `structural_independent_of_ranker` adds what does not depend on the ranker: the root, the start of a day, the view and the depth are the same in two contexts that agree on the hash function, the harness's name and the knobs, whatever their rankers, embedders, recipe times, tool names and writer names. It compares one memory read in two contexts. It does not say that two rankers build the same memory from the same operations, and they need not: a lookup's return is an info of the log, and what the writer keeps of it is listed by the next root.

### Theorem 4: bounded serving

Every return carries at most the cap in tokens of data and at most the cap in pointers; a served closure is paged by `Params.page` (the cap less the arrival number a return opens with), nothing lost, in `⌈length / page⌉` pages; the closure itself terminates and is the closure of section 2; and a span is served page by page, in pieces that put end to end are exactly its part of the info's canonical form, with a cursor that reaches the end in at most `⌈length / page⌉` pages. `root_lines_bounded`: the root shows at most `k` lines and `c` keeps whatever the record's size (lines, not tokens: the window is not modelled). The paging of a closure (`serve`) is a function: no tool serves a closure, and a lookup by words serves the first page of its stream (the first page of a span lookup at the tool is its cursor's first step, `span_cursor_is_serveN`; that a later lookup resumes where a cursor stopped is shown on one instance by `nonvacuous` and stated by no theorem; a words lookup has no continuation: see the findings).

### Theorem 5: append-only

Every operation is a function from log to log by appending; no info changes. Every operation yields a memory that extends the one it was applied to; an accepted append adds the info to the end of one log and changes nothing else; a change to an info that has a successor in its log leaves a memory that is not well-formed (`tamper_evident_local`). A local edit only: since the hash no longer covers the history pointer a held tail hash pins nothing before it (`tail_hash_not_commit` exhibits two well-formed memories with different toolkits and the same tail hash), and a tail rewritten with consistent history pointers is not detected. What a held list of the log's hashes pins is `log_hashes_commit`.

### T6 to T10: lookup

```lean
theorem lexical_complete (Γ : Ctx) (m : Memory) (s : Scope) (words : Data) (p : Policy) :
    ∀ x ∈ m.scopeInfos s, x.holdsWords words = true → x ∈ lookupWords Γ m s words p

theorem floor_not_a_loss (Γ : Ctx) (m : Memory) (hwf : WellFormed Γ m) (s : Scope) :
    ∀ x ∈ m.scopeInfos s, lookupPtr m s x.hash = [x]
```

T6 is completeness only: no precision (a word can match a bookkeeping token such as an arrival number) and no ranking. `fuse` is a concatenation with de-duplication, not a rank-score fusion: T7 says what fusion cannot lose, not that it orders well. T8 is not "the deployed ranker is deterministic", which no model of the log can state: the ranker is a Lean function of the memory, the words and the policy, and the key of the policy (its three parts) identifies the policy (`policy_key_injective`), so what is proved is that the policies of a log are a chain (`policy_chain`), that a lookup's call points to the policy it ran under (`recall_records_policy`), and that its return is recomputed from the log before the call under that policy, whatever policies came after (`lookup_replayable`, for memories the harness reached). T9's second part is the definition of the canonical form (`Info.canon` opens with the pointer, the envelope and the arrival number); the point is that the return carries them (`lookup_return_exact`). T10 holds because a lookup by a pointer consults no ranker.

### The coverage hypothesis

```lean
def Coverage (Γ : Ctx) (W : Wanted) (m : Memory) (p : Policy) : Prop :=
  ∀ (s : Scope) (words : Data), ∀ x ∈ m.scopeInfos s, W words x → x.holdsWords words = false → x ∈ Γ.ranker m words p

theorem combined_of_coverage (Γ : Ctx) (W : Wanted) (m : Memory) (p : Policy) (hc : Coverage Γ W m p) (s : Scope)
    (words : Data) : ∀ x ∈ m.scopeInfos s, W words x → x ∈ lookupWords Γ m s words p
```

`Wanted` is what the writer would want by these words: the meaning, which no theorem about the log can see, and which is not formalised. Coverage is a named hypothesis, never an axiom: it is a fact about the embedder and the corpus. The guarantee is conditional on it, by T6 for the exact matches and by coverage for the rest. The guarantee is exactly coverage: `coverage_iff` proves that `Coverage` holds if and only if everything the writer would want is among the candidates, so the conditional theorem is the hypothesis restated with T6 added, and it is a statement about the candidate list, not about the pages a call serves. The hypothesis is not empty (`coverage_satisfiable`: a ranker that returns the whole memory covers every meaning; `nonvacuous` has a ranker that is neither empty nor total whose own criterion is the meaning it covers: both witnesses are degenerate, neither exhibits coverage for a semantic ranker), and it is needed (`coverage_needed`: with a ranker that returns nothing there is a well-formed memory in which the writer would want an info that the lookup does not return, so the combined guarantee fails and coverage fails with it).

### T11, T13, T14: the tools

`toolStep_wellFormed`: every call of every tool, valid or not, leaves a well-formed memory well-formed. `toolStep_accepts`: a valid call (`ToolCall.Valid`: ready, and its needs met) records its experience and serves a return that is not a refusal, first pointer the call, written by the tool, on the day of the call. `toolStep_chain0`: a call is a chain of accepted pushes that opens no day, so every intermediate memory is well-formed (what the replay theorems of `Conformance.lean` need).

The time of a call (T13) is the day and the machine, never the window (section 18c): `toolStep_returns_same_day` says every call, valid or not, leaves a new return in the private store on the day of the call and before any new root; `lookup_one_capped_return` that a lookup is one call, one return and one cursor; `act_needs_declared_bound` that an act on a recipe whose list declares no time bound is refused; `ticks_charged_le_declared` that the time charged is within the declared bound (by definition of the cut `min recipeTime bound`, since the model has no clock); `act_effect` says the outcome and the return record that cut time. No theorem claims that a trace or a thread terminates: the writer may decline to continue, and the chains are finite inputs of the call. T14: `chain_links_are_threaded` (a link is in the thread of the link before it), `consider_continues_target` (a consider of an entry appends a link and a continues edge from the link to the entry, and the link is in the entry's thread), `chain_head_reachable` (the head of each trace a consider returns is an entry, not retired, knowledge at the next start of day, and reached from the next root computed right after the call, through the head of its thread, in at most `depth + 1` hops: an unfinished chain is reachable the day after the call).

### T12: the cost of traversal

The hops from the next root to any head are at most `depth + 1`, each stepping from an info of at most `hopBound = 6 + rootBound` tokens; under the knob condition `RootFitsPage` (`hopBound ≤ page`, a named hypothesis on the knobs) each hop is one capped return, a lookup by the pointer of the info. The cost is counted in hops and pages: the model has no clock.

### Conformance

- `roots_prescribed`: every root in a memory the harness reached is `root Γ m₀` for the memory `m₀` the harness had when it started that day.
- `replay_log_derivable`: replaying the infos of a memory the harness reached, in arrival order, offered to their logs, rebuilds exactly that memory. This is consistency, not authenticity (finding 36). The sentence "replaying the log of every well-formed memory rebuilds it" is false, and this is proved (`replay_log_false`: for any context with keep cap 1 there is a well-formed memory it fails on): the keep cap of invariant 7 is a property of the whole memory, and a supersedes edge that arrives later can bring the memory back under the cap after an earlier keep was over it. It holds for a well-formed memory whose every cut by arrival number keeps the cap (`replay_log_of_keeps`), which every memory the harness reached satisfies (`derivable_keepsCapped`).
- `lookup_replayable`: in a memory the harness reached, the first page of the return to a recorded lookup by words is recomputed from the log before the call, the words the call recorded and the policy its derivation points to (its own epoch: later policies change nothing); `ret` must be a return (`lookup_replayable_false`: an edge offered to the private store under a tool's name would otherwise satisfy every other hypothesis).
- `Memory.ops` finds the infos of a memory by arrival number, one `find?` each, so that the kernel computes it; on a memory with well-formed arrival numbers it is the infos in arrival order.
- `log_hashes_commit`, and the first version's `log_committed` (a held tail hash pins the whole log) is false here: `tail_hash_not_commit`, for any hash function.

### The theorems are not about an empty class

`Nonvacuous.lean` gives `nonvacuous`: a concrete context (an injective hash function, proved injective, `k = 2`, `c = 2`, `cap = 8`, a ranker that returns the infos sharing a word with the query) and a list of 36 operations whose replay is a well-formed memory of 85 infos that the harness reaches, and in which:

- all ten tools are called, and each call has its return, written by the tool, that is not a refusal and points first to the call;
- there are returns of kind infos, span, nothing, digest and refusal (one by a tool, one by the harness), and a cursor; a lookup by a span starts where a cursor stopped;
- a lookup by words returns an info that does not hold the words and that only the ranker found (the words are not empty, and the ranker, applied to the memory before the call, returns it under the policy the call points to, a policy info of the shared store that decodes); the call recorded the words;
- there is a chain of two policy epochs written by the desk (epoch 1 points to epoch 0 and degrades the ranker), and two lookups by the same words, the first under epoch 0 and the second under epoch 1, whose derivations point to their own epoch and whose returns differ (the section 18g point is that the same recorded lookup replayed under both epochs returns different things, and that replay uses its own; the conjunct itself states only that the two returns differ, and the log also grew between the two lookups);
- the coverage hypothesis holds, for the policy of a policy info of the log, for a meaning that names an info the lexical side misses and the ranker finds, while the ranker leaves out another info of the scope;
- there is a day of work (a page headed by a task, a call, every task-carrying info of the day pointing to the task, and a result, the info filed last that points to the task), on which a night is written after the hand-over, an experience a day may still take after its end, as a stop may under amendment B;
- there is a day of recreation (a page with no task, a call, a filing, and an experience that points to nothing);
- there is a sub-frame that opens a sub-frame (an aside opened by a consider's call, and an aside opened by that aside);
- there is a thread continued across two days: an entry that was a head of the memory at the end of the first day, and, the next day, a consider naming it whose aside is joined to it by a continues edge, so that the aside is in its thread;
- the grouping fallback fired (a group node stands), an entry is retired by a supersedes edge of the hippocampus, and a keep stands.

`WellFormed` of that memory is decided by the kernel (`decide +kernel`, Lean's own checker). The witness is a proof that the statements are satisfiable together, not a claim about any model's behaviour. `RootFitsPage` is false on its knobs (`hopBound` is 20, a page is 7); `rootFitsPage_satisfiable` shows it satisfiable on others. `retired_entry_unreachable` is a second concrete memory in which a retired entry lies out of reach of the root.

### Counterexamples that are proved

Each shows that a hypothesis or a design choice is load-bearing.

- `literal_reachability_false`: not every info is reachable from the root.
- `replay_log_false`: not every well-formed memory is rebuilt by replaying its log (keep cap 1).
- `tail_hash_not_commit`: a held tail hash does not commit the log.
- `coverage_needed`: without the coverage hypothesis the combined guarantee fails.
- `push_bounded_counterexample`, `push_bounded_false` (`Lemmas/PushCex.lean`): `push_bounded` needs `Resolves m`, because a dangling supersedes pointer can name a keep that is not yet written.
- `lookup_replayable_false` (`Lemmas/ConformAux4.lean`): `lookup_replayable` needs `ret` to be a return.
- `considerTraces_spec_false` (`Tools/Consider.lean`): a consider's traces are accepted only on a day that has not ended.
- `retired_entry_unreachable` (`Nonvacuous.lean`): a retired entry can lie out of reach of the root.

## Rulings of the desk, made while the first version was built (2026-09-24)

1. The extra pointer on the hippocampus edge and keep rows is dropped: an edge carries exactly two pointers, a keep exactly one.
2. Retirement without a replacement does not exist: a shelf item or tool is retired only by a supersedes edge from its replacement; when nothing replaces it, the desk appends a withdrawal notice and the edge points from the notice.
3. The pointer order is the same for every edge kind: (subject, object), "A supersedes B". The retired info is the second pointer.
4. A root is appended each day (definition 30), not each roll.
5. Definition 43 wins: framing rows in both parts of the store.

## Rulings of section 15, which are the scope of this version

Numbers are those of section 15. 1: entries are the derived infos of every kind but edge, keep, root, group, page, day record, return and cursor. 2 and 3: the grouping fallback fires whenever the heads exceed `k`, every day, over all heads. 4 and 6: definitions 49 and 48 corrected. 5: an aside points to the consider call's experience. 7: serving appends a return and a cursor for each page. 8: a `heard` row has one pointer, to the say line copied into the shared store. 9: the first root has no pointer, every return points to its call. 10: the hash covers data and envelope; the chaining pointer and the arrival number are outside it. 11: retirement is permanent, and a retired continues edge no longer joins threads. 12: invariant 9. 13: invariant 11. 14: invariant 10. 15: knowledge is the derived infos the model made, not retired, in the closure of the root. 16: the keep cap is a property of the whole memory.

## Choices where the records are silent, or where the reading was forced

- Each info holds a pointer to the info before it in its log (18) and an arrival number. The hash covers the content: data, envelope and the derivation pointers. Ruling 10 says "data and envelope only" (definition 14); the derivation is also in the hash here, because two infos with the same data and envelope but different derivations would otherwise collide under invariant 1's distinct hashes (for example two answers of one day to different questions). If the desk rules the pointers out, an unnumbered kind must then differ in data.
- Every kind the harness or a tool builds is numbered: its data opens with its own arrival number, so that two of them never share a hash. Not numbered: a night, a root, a page, and what the desk and the readers place (notice, framing, shelf item, answer, task, say, heard, tool declaration).
- `append` takes the log to append to: an edge of kind supersedes is a row of all four logs, so the destination is not a function of the info.
- The day id of an info is the number of roots up to and including it (the first day is 1). Nothing else marks a day in the model.
- The keep cap is applied by refusing the `(c + 1)`th standing keep, not by dropping older ones from the root.
- An info's size is its data plus one token per pointer. A return holds at most the cap in data and at most the cap in pointers, so at most twice the cap (definition 36: a fixed number). A served stream is paged by `Params.page = cap - 1`, because a return's data opens with its arrival number.
- The root shows one line for each top item: its id and the first `titleCap` tokens of its data. A group node is bounded by invariant 7: at most `k` pointers and two tokens of data (its arrival number and its size); without a bound on its data a well-formed memory could hold a harness group node of any length and T12 would fail (found and proved by a reviewer; the bound is the repair).
- The closure of section 2 (over derivations and relation edges, in either direction) is used for knowledge; pointer chains (definition 26) count hops in T2 and T12.
- The kinds of a call (`call`, `recipe`, `given`, `outcome`) are this model's own and are not entries: they are the bookkeeping of a tool, and were they entries every call would be a head. `question`, `hand-over` and `stop` are entries.
- The day ends at a hand-over or a stop, and only the night or a stop can follow it: the night is written as the day closes whatever ended it (section 13), and under amendment B (below) the individual's stop line may follow too. This reads definitions 59 and 60 ("the day's last experience") together with the night; it is a choice.
- Night arity is any: on a day of work it carries the task; on a day of recreation it may carry nothing, and then it is not derived, so it is not an entry and not knowledge.
- An edge and a keep have their arity fixed by definitions 24 and 27 and are exempt from carrying the task pointer that definition 66 asks of every experience of a work day (a `keeping` with no target points at the call, which carries the task).
- A refusal the harness records for an offer points to the newest info of the private store, not to a call (there is none); with an empty private store it points to nothing. A refusal of a tool call that the memory recorded points to the call. Reasons: the number of the invariant (1 to 11 and 13); 20, no such tool declared; 21, a return the memory would not take; 31 and more (30 + n), a tool's own.
- A `consider` appends a continues edge from its first link to any entry it is given, not only to heads; a consider of an older entry forks the thread and the new link becomes its head.
- The words in a lookup are tokens: a word matches when it is a token of the info's data (the arrival number included); an empty query holds nothing, so a lookup by no words has no lexical candidates.
- Recall and reach are scoped: recall to the hippocampus, reach to the private and shared parts of the store. The toolkit (tool declarations and their withdrawals) is outside both, so no tool reaches a toolkit info by its pointer; `Memory.resolve` does (T2 part 4 is about that function).
- A policy is an info of the shared store (`Kind.policy`, design record section 18g): its data is the policy key (lexical index version, embedder hash, anchor hashes) and the reason it replaced the one before; it has at most one pointer, to that one; the desk writes it (any name but the model's). The policy in force is the latest one of the log; epochs are what the pointers trace back from it (`policy_chain` gives a forest: see the end of this item). A lookup by words records its words in the call, and the call points to the policy it ran under; a lookup by a pointer or a span records the query and consults no policy. `Policy.lexVersion` is consumed by nothing (the lexical side is one fixed function); the embedder hash and the anchors reach only the opaque ranker. Nothing requires the chain to be linear (a second policy with no pointer is accepted; the latest by arrival is in force), and nothing checks the reason or that the anchors name shared-store infos.
- A return holds the first page of its stream and points to at most `cap - 1` of the infos it re-presents; the digest of a consider is, for each trace, the first `titleCap` tokens of its last link, with a pointer to that last link (the head of the trace), not the first lines of every aside (definition 55, read with section 18c).
- A cursor is appended for every return a tool serves, whatever its kind. The refusal that the harness records for a refused offer has none (`step_refused` appends exactly one info). A recipe is a number; the list of recipes is the latest info of kind `shelf recipes` in the shared store, a flat list of pairs (recipe, declared time bound); an act's data is given inline, not named by a pointer. An act that runs over its declared bound is recorded with the cut time and acknowledged, not refused.
- A hand-over, a stop and a question on a day of work carry the task, as every experience does; they are entries (heads of the model's own words to the reader). A day with no page at all binds nothing, like a day of recreation.
- The room's other individuals are not modelled: `filed` is written by the model itself into the shared store, so "for others to reach" is the shared part being readable, and `reach` returns what the model has seen or what it filed.

14. Definition 8 and "a night at the end of every day" were not enforced. Ruled (14): invariant 10, one night a day and none before the first root; the day's token bound is not enforced.
15. Definition 31 against section 2. Ruled (15). Resolved.
16. The keep cap is not prefix-closed. Ruled (16): with invariant 2 a dangling supersedes pointer is refused. Both consequences stand as proved counterexamples: `replay_log_false`, and, for the local check taken alone, `push_bounded_counterexample` (`Lemmas/PushCex.lean`).

New in this version, not ruled, listed for the desk:

17. The design record's section 14 numbers "a filed info is never placed unasked" invariant 9, and section 17 numbers retirement 9. Here retirement is 9, the filed rule is a consequence of 11, the work-day rule is 13, and 12 is the index.
18. Definition 66 says every experience of a work day carries a pointer to the task; definitions 24 and 27 fix an edge at two pointers and a keep at one. The model exempts edge and keep.
19. Definitions 59 and 60 make the hand-over and the stop "the day's last experience"; section 13 makes the night the end of every day. The model lets the night follow, and, with amendment B, a stop.
20. `ruling 10` says the hash covers data and envelope only; the model also covers the derivation pointers (see the choices).
21. The cursor is a numbered kind, so its data opens with its arrival number: a cursor's data is that number and then the four fields of `Cursor.toData` (target, start, length, served). Found by the prover of the theorem that ties the tool's cursor to `Cursor.serveN`, whose first statement forgot the number.
22. Definition 57 says the reader is the one a framing names; any name is accepted. Definition 65 says a task names the recipes that may be used; the model does not enforce it. Definition 64 says an act answers with infos, an acknowledgement or a refusal; the model's act answers with an acknowledgement (or a refusal), because the output of a recipe is not modelled.
23. `Pinned` (a ranker that depends on the policy only through its key) was a hypothesis in the first draft of this version; the key determines the policy, so the hypothesis holds for every ranker and is dropped: T8 says what a Lean function of the policy can say. Determinism of a deployed ranker (approximate nearest neighbours, floating point, threads) is not something the log can carry.
24. The frames of definitions 42 to 50 (`Header`, `Frame`, `RootFrame`, `SubFrame`) are defined and used by no invariant and no theorem: what enters a context is a read. Invariant 6 and `filed_never_placed` concern the page (a kind) and its pointers, not a frame.
25. `Refusal` reasons of a tool are offset from the invariants' numbers (a tool's reason 1 is recorded as 31), so that a reason never names an invariant it did not break.

26. `filed_never_placed` is about pages. The kind tables let a root or a group point at a kind that only entries should be (`Kind.isEntryKind` is a property of a kind, and `entries` are hippocampus infos): a well-formed memory the harness did not reach could hold a root over a filed info. The roots and groups the harness places are built from heads, groups and keeps only.
27. A lookup by words serves its first page and no tool continues it: the cursor of a words lookup names the call, and the call's own canonical form is not the stream. The first page of a span lookup is its cursor's first step (`span_cursor_is_serveN`); the cursor of a pointer lookup is stated only in an internal lemma (`LookupAux.lookup_shape`); no theorem says that a later lookup resumes where a cursor stopped. The paging of T4 and T7 is proved for the function, not for a tool of words.
28. Definition 55 says the digest is the asides' first lines; the digest here is one line per trace (its last link), which is what the trace's head is.
29. The design record's sentence for T10, "every info is reached by its name in one lookup", holds for every info of the scopes of the two lookup tools; toolkit infos are reached by `Memory.resolve` only.

30. The canonical form of an info (`Info.canon`: hash, writer, day, kind code, arrival number, the number of pointers, the pointers, the data) has no length for the data, so it is not self-delimiting for a reader who cannot check hashes: the tail of a seen info's data can imitate the header of another info. The provenance of T9 holds for a reader that verifies hashes.
31. The graph functions (`Memory.edges`, threads, the closure, heads) ignore who wrote an edge and in which log. A `continues` edge offered by the desk or a reader to the private store joins threads and can change the heads of the next root (the model can retire it with its own supersedes edge). Section 3 says the writer changes the root only by writing new entries; section 17 says every edge is a writer's own act. Neither is enforced.
32. The model may retire store items and its own tool declarations with `relate` of kind supersedes (invariant 9 binds only edges whose target is in the hippocampus): a tool retired that way is refused by the harness until the desk declares it again. Ruling 2 (no retirement without a replacement) is not enforced: the source of a supersedes edge may be any info. `Memory.recipeList` ignores retirement (`Memory.toolDecl` does not).
33. The work-day rule binds only on a day that has a page. A page appended after the day's experiences can only be taskless (a page that names a task is refused if an experience of the day lacks it), so a work day can become a day of recreation after the fact. On a day of recreation, a night that cites an old task is an entry, and a pointer-free night, question, hand-over or stop is not.
34. The night may come at any time of the day and is optional; a `newDay` before the night loses that day's night; a correction, a consolidation and a proposal are accepted at any time (section 13 puts the consolidation at the roll).
35. `ToolCall.Valid` is the premise of acceptance and is not enforced: an invalid consider (an empty chain, fewer chains than targets) is recorded with a degenerate digest. A consider of more than `cap - 1` targets is valid, since `Needs` carries no cap for it: it appends a trace for each target, and its digest lists `cap - 1` heads, so the heads beyond that are in no return of the call.
36. `replay_log_derivable` and `roots_prescribed` say a log the harness produced is consistent and its roots are the prescribed ones. A forged but well-formed log replays the same way (`Derivable` is not a predicate on the log): "the log certifies itself" is self-consistency, not authenticity.
37. A consider of an older entry makes its new link the head of that thread and can demote the tip of an earlier chain from the heads. The tip stays knowledge through the closure, reached through relation edges, which no tool lets the writer follow.
38. Design section 12's rumination guard (a trace is read only by `recall` with the aside's id) and section 5's "the root lists the open readings" are not modelled: a `recall` by words returns asides, and a cursor is a private-store info that no root line reaches (definition 30 wins: the root holds the day id, the heads and the keeps).
39. A hash is one token in every bound (`Hash` and `Token` are naturals): the real width of a hash scales `rootBound`, `RootFitsPage` and the cap.
40. Ruling 10's "one hash in every memory" holds for the same writer, kind, data, derivation and day id: the envelope's day id is per memory.
41. Against section 13: a correction is accepted without the corrects edge the harness would append; nothing produces a day record; the page and the day record are the harness's only by name (`WritersOk` is one way, finding on invariant 5); `Policy.anchors` is data that nothing checks against the shared store, and a policy is accepted from any name but the model's.
42. A refusal the harness records points to the newest private-store info, whatever offer or call was refused, and the log does not say which; reason 21 is unreachable (a return never fails).
43. `Params.hcap` allows `cap = 2`, a page of one token: a lookup by a pointer then returns only the arrival number and the hash, and an act's return is cut to the recipe (`act_effect` states the cut form). T9's "each with its pointer, envelope and place" in page 1 needs a page of six tokens; a words lookup's first page is filled by the lexical candidates first, so the vector side can be displaced entirely.

## Amendment B (a candidate, 2026-10-02)

This branch is a candidate for a new version of the model, built and proved locally on the desk lead's decision of 2026-10-02 ("B"). It is not a published version: `main` stays at the version above until the desk lead names the publication.

What changes. The hippocampus takes a stop line offered by the individual: `Kind.offerableIn` gains `(hippocampus, stop)`. Invariant 10 lets a stop, as the night, follow a hand-over or a stop of the same day: the third clause of `DaysOk` concludes `i.kind = .night ∨ i.kind = .stop`, and the third clause of `LocDays` exempts a stop as it exempts the night. No other definition changes, and no theorem is restated: seven proofs change, and only in their bodies: four receive the new premise of `LocDays` (`i.kind ≠ .stop`) as one more argument (`ok_hip`, `call_ok_tc`, `contEdge_ok`, `aside_ok`); two discharge it for a kind that is not a stop (`ended_day_takes_no_calls`, `considerTraces_spec_false`); and `push_days` proves the new disjunct of `DaysOk` and the new premise of `LocDays` against each other; and every statement stands as it was.

What was decided, and why.

- The tool `stop` stays as it is: the declared route, with its call and its return. The stop line is the route that needs no declaration. Invariant 5 still keeps the hippocampus the individual's: a line under any other name is refused.
- A stop line is a `stop` info like the tool's: numbered (its data opens with its arrival number), pointing only to a task (`Kind.targetOk`), with no first-pointer constraint (`Kind.firstOk`), and on a day of work it carries the task (invariant 13). No call of its own precedes it (other calls may stand before it), so a `stop` info no longer implies an accepted call of the tool; no theorem of this model stated that it did.
- A stop line ends the day as the tool's stop does: `Memory.dayEnded` reads the kind.
- A stop line may carry a payload: its data, after the arrival number, is the individual's own and is not read. "At will and without a reason" means that no reason is asked, not that none may be given (audit F, F6; the DA's ruling 3 of that audit).
- More than one stop may stand on a day: a stop may follow a stop. Forbidding it would need a clause of its own, and a second stop changes nothing the first did not.

What it carries of its own (`MemoryArtifact/StopLine.lean`):

- `ok_stopLine`: on a well-formed memory on which a day has been lived, the individual's stop line (`Ctx.lineDraft`: writer the individual, kind `stop`, today's task as its pointers) passes every local check in the hippocampus, with the tool `stop` declared or not, before or after the end of the day.
- `stopLine_effect`: taking it appends exactly that info to the hippocampus, and the day has then ended. `stopLine_derivable`: the harness reaches the memory after it from every memory it reaches.
- `Nonvac.stopLine_witnesses`, decided by the kernel on the witness of `Nonvacuous.lean`: the line taken on a first day with no tool declared; taken after day 1's hand-over (a day of work: it points to the task); taken after day 2's call of the tool `stop` (day 2 then holds two stops); and on day 2, open, the line taken, a call of `recall` after it refused, and the night still taken. Each run is one the harness performs.
- A limit (audit F, finding F6): on a day whose page names no task, the check does not forbid a stop that points to an earlier day's task; the line the harness drafts carries none. `Nonvac.stopLine_limit` pins it: on day 2 of the witness the line points to nothing, and a stop pointing to day 1's task is taken. Closing it changes invariant 11 and is left to the artifact's next version.
- `Nonvac.stopLine_refusals`, decided by the kernel: a stop offered before the first day (invariant 10), under the desk's name (invariant 5), on a day of work without the task (invariant 13), and with data that does not open with its arrival number (invariant 1) is refused, by the first invariant named.
- Without `(hippocampus, stop)` in `Kind.offerableIn` this file does not build.

What pins the two tables (`MemoryArtifact/Tables.lean`, after audit F, finding F1): `offerableIn_table` decides the whole of `Kind.offerableIn`, for every log and every kind, against a table written out there, and `targetOk_table` decides the whole of `Kind.targetOk` the same way. So a change to either definition fails the build unless the table changes with it: an offered hand-over, an offered aside, a stop offered to the shared store, or a stop that points to any kind but a task. `Kind.all` lists the 43 kinds (`Kind.mem_all`). The tables were generated from the definitions, so they pin what the definitions say today, not what the design record says.

A note on naming. The design record calls "the bare line stop:" the line by which the individual calls the tool `stop` (the pass notes on tool lines). The stop line of amendment B is the memory's side of the same act when no tool call is possible: an offer of a `stop` info, with no call and no return. Which of the two the harness performs for a given line of the individual's is the harness's choice and outside this model. The `stop` info alone does not tell them apart (a line with empty data has the shape of the tool's stop): the call and the return that stand before the tool's stop and not before a line are where a reader looks, and no theorem here states that they suffice.

What it amends in the records: definition 60 (the stop, now also the individual's line, and not only the day's last experience); definitions 59 and 60 read with section 13 as finding 19 does (after the end of a day the night or a stop may follow); ruling 14 (invariant 10); section 13's append catalogue (who may append a stop, and that the hippocampus takes it offered); and section 14's closed list of tools, which no longer holds every route to a stop.

## What is not proved, and why

- Coverage, that the embedder finds what the writer means. It is a named hypothesis; only measurement can settle it.
- The quality of any ranker or of rank fusion. `fuse` is concatenation; no claim about relevance or order within a side.
- Determinism of a real ranker (index seeds, floats, GPU, threads). The model's ranker is a Lean function, so this is true by type and says nothing about the deployed one.
- Anything about the embedder itself, its file hash, the anchors being in the shared store, cross-model geometry. All opaque or unmodelled. The anchor set is data in the policy. The probe set and the regression test of section 18g (a candidate policy is run over recorded lookups and adopted only if it does not return fewer of the judged-relevant items) are not modelled: the desk writes a policy and the model accepts it, and no theorem claims a policy is better than the one before.
- That the index is derived from the log. It holds by definition of `derivedIndex`; a real index is not an object of the model. Privacy of the index: no secrecy statement exists.
- That only the model's own output reaches its hippocampus. The `writer` field is a label; the model accepts any offer that carries it.
- Reader honesty, the truth of notices, answers and tasks, and whether a task's permitted recipes are obeyed.
- What the model writes or learns: nights, asides, chain contents, uptake by a base model, whether it continues or declines a thread.
- Termination of any trace or thread. Not claimed and not claimable: the writer may decline, and the chains of a call are finite inputs.
- Wall-clock time, the machine, disk and RAM, and "only time grows". The model has no clock. "Within the day" means only that no root comes between a call and its return, and the recipe's bound is enforced by a `min`.
- That recipes halt or what they output. `Ctx.recipeTime` is a total function.
- Day size in tokens, the window, the context, the week, the roll, hydration and the burn-in cache have no Lean counterpart; the headers, frames, root-frame, meta-frame and sub-frame are defined and no theorem uses them.
- Bounds on sibling frames (`m`, `t`) and the digest: replaced by section 18c; only the return cap bounds the digest.
- That a night exists every day, and that a reader's answer sits at the head of the next day's page.
- The room language (`same:`, `continue:`, `correct:` with its edge), typify, the ledger lines.
- Sharing between individuals. The shared store is a per-memory copy.
- The paging of a closure (`serve`) and of a words lookup by a tool: no tool serves a closure, and a words lookup has no continuation.
- Cryptographic hash security. `Hasher.injective` assumes no collisions. The tokenizer and the real bytes are outside the model.
- Tamper detection against an adversary who rewrites a log tail consistently. Only local edits are caught.
- That the harness in production conforms to the model. Conformance is by tests against the model's laws (design record section 9).
- That the kind-target and first-pointer tables are what the desk intends. They are enforced, not justified.
- The paper's claim that models work. Not a Lean matter.
